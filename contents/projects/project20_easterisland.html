A Spatially Explicit Agent-Based Model of Human-Resource Interaction on Easter Island

Master thesis project on socio-environmental modelling

at the Leibinz-Institute for Tropical Marine Science (ZMT) and KTH Stockholm

2020

Collaborators and Supervisors

  • Agostino Merico (Supervisor)
  • Michael Hanke (Examiner at KTH)

Summary

We develop an Agent-Based model incorporating real geography, individual behaviors, and dynamic agent-environment interactions to explore Easter Island's pre-European settlement and deforestation patterns. This approach allows testing hypotheses about spatio-temporal dynamics that previous models overlooked.

Full abstract: The history of Easter Island, with its cultural and ecological mysteries, has attracted the interests of archaeologists, anthropologists, ecologists, and economists alike. Despite great scientific efforts, uncertainties in the available data leave a number of issues unsolved and open to debate. The maximum size reached by the human population before the arrival of Europeans and the spatio-temporal dynamics of deforestation are some of the aspects still fraught with controversies. By providing a workbench for testing hypotheses and scenarios, mathematical models are valuable complements to archaeological studies. Previous models, however, omitted potentially important drivers such as characteristics and behaviours of individuals and environmental heterogeneities. Here we present an Agent-Based model characterised by a real island geography, individual agent decision-making processes, and co-evolving agent-environment interactions. We use the model to determine plausible spatial and temporal patterns of deforestation, settlement, and land use prior to European contact. We find that these patterns can vary qualitatively from region to region. Regional variations become pronounced not due to environmental heterogeneities alone but also when agents factor-in knowledge of the environment in their moving decisions. Our findings suggest that Easter Island's past may be more complex than what a simple island-wide narrative can capture.
