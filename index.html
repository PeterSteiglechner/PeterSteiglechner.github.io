About Me

We live in a time of deep political and societal transformation driven by contested questions about how to mitigate or adapt to climate change, reduce growing inequality, manage migration and foster diversity. And we observe growing polarisation, increasingly identity-driven discourse, and a rise in authoritarian sentiment. What drives these trends? And how do our individual beliefs shape—and get shaped by—their social environments?

The goal of my research is to better understand the interplay of perception, identity, and communication in shaping collective opinion patterns (and people's perceptions of those patterns). For this I combine mathematical modelling, agent-based simulations, and data science.

Currently, I am interested in belief systems: how belief systems provide people with feelings of coherence or dissonance when they face new information, how people adapt their belief systems in social environments, and how the structure of belief systems can grow increasingly different between individuals. In sum, I aim to describe how changing and heterogeneous belief systems contribute to societal patterns like consensus, fragmentation, or perceived polarisation.

Previously, I explored how cognitive biases (e.g., biases related to social identity or confirmation bias) and ambiguous communication affect societal opinion dynamics — sometimes in quite unexpected ways. Before shifting to the social sciences, I had studied physics and applied mathematics and did some work on climate modelling. I was always and continue to be fascinated by the fuzzy overlap between the natural and social sciences regarding the science of climate change.

Research Projects

Heterogeneous & adaptive belief networks

Modelling study

People can change both beliefs and the relationships they perceive between beliefs when they experience social or internal dissonance. Do people then develop different belief system structures? What are the consequences of this, for example for the effectiveness of belief interventions or for perceptions of polarisation? We provide insights by modelling this co-evolution of beliefs and belief networks.

ongoing

How do people perceive political distances to others?

Survey and Data Analysis

Scientists often assume that people map opinion disagreement to a general feeling of political distance in an objective and symmetric way. But is this true? We test this assumption and hypothesise that specific individuals weigh disagreement on some topics higher than on others, and that people do not use the same mapping for this.

ongoing

Perceived polarisation

Modelling and Data Analysis

The opinion distribution of a person's in-group shapes how that person perceives disagreement in the overall society. When groups grow more uniform, opposing views seem more extreme, making polarisation feel greater to them than actual opinion divergence suggests. We formalise this idea and analyse how Germans might have perceived polarisation on climate change opinions and how these perceptions can deviate across groups and vary over time.

The Interplay of Ambiguity and Bias in Opinion Dynamics

Modelling and Literature Review

Different types of noise affect opinion dynamics in quite different ways: increase polarisation, promote agreement through compromise, but even promote more extreme consensus, given the right conditions. Noise is often overlooked or insufficiently dealt with in social dynamics models but it can be critical and surprising.

[Paper in Royal Society Open Science, Opinion Piece "Make some noise!"]

Social Identity Bias and Polarisation

Modelling study

We model how social identity biases undermine how individuals update opinions through social influence. The model illustrates that these biases can either hinder or, quite counter-intuitively, promote consensus, and that this depends on how structured the interaction networks both within and between groups are.

[Paper in Royal Society Interface]

Modelling population dynamics and deforestation patterns on Easter Island

Masterthesis on socio-environmental modelling @ ZMT

We develop an Agent-Based model incorporating real geography, individual behaviours, and dynamic agent-environment interactions to explore Easter Island's pre-European settlement and deforestation patterns. This approach allows testing hypotheses about spatio-temporal dynamics that previous models overlooked.

[Thesis, Book Chapter]

Global Warming through anthropogenic heat flux

Masterthesis on climate modelling @ PIK

Anthropogenic heat describes any type of energy that humans use and add to the energy balance of the earth system, i.e. energy that would not become heat anyway. This includes fossil fuels, but also any form of energy from nuclear fusion/fission. I estimate the climate impacts of this anthropogenic heat flux using climate models, including temperature anomalies and its feedbacks with ice melt and ocean circulation.

[Thesis]
